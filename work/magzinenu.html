The brief

Building the future of Dutch publishing — before anyone else did

The iPad launched in 2010. Wob ten Hoeve had been thinking about this moment since a Dutch publishers' congress in 2009. He saw it clearly: physical magazine distribution was entering a slow decline, and the publishers who moved first to digital would own the transition. MagZine.nu was his bet — the Netherlands' first independent digital magazine kiosk, positioned to serve publishers and readers equally.

By 2012, the platform needed to grow beyond its first-generation app. The kiosk had early traction and a compelling vision, but the technical foundation needed to be rebuilt for scale — cleaner architecture, richer features, and a platform capable of evolving as publishing itself evolved. That's when TBox came in.

The partnership

TBox became MagZine.nu's engineering partner in 2012 and stayed for over a decade. Not as a vendor who handed over code. As the team that owned the build, understood the product deeply, and evolved it through multiple major versions, a white-label product line, and a subscription service — all while the platform served hundreds of thousands of Dutch readers.

Product 01 — Main platform

MagZine.nu: The Digital Kiosk

The main MagZine.nu app was the flagship. TBox built it from the ground up for iPad, then extended it to iPhone and Android — making it one of the earliest fully multi-platform magazine experiences in the Netherlands. At peak, the app served over 400 Dutch-language titles and recorded 500,000+ annual downloads.

This wasn't a simple reader app. It was a full-featured magazine commerce platform — with online/offline download management, secure subscription flows, in-app purchase integrations, push notifications, and a reading experience polished to the standards of a consumer product that needed to compete with apps from major European publishers.

Product 02 — White-label platform

AppVilla: One Engine, Many Brands

As MagZine.nu grew, publishers didn't just want to be listed in the kiosk — they wanted their own branded app. A standalone product, carrying their masthead, published under their own App Store account. Wob ten Hoeve saw the opportunity: build a white-label product that could be instantly rebranded and deployed for any publisher, without duplicating engineering effort.

TBox built that product. The AppVilla platform was a configurable, white-labelled mobile application — one underlying codebase that could be dressed as any publisher's branded magazine app. Each deployment had its own in-app purchases, its own subscription products, its own backend API connections, and its own visual identity.

ARCHITECTURE How the white-label system worked

A single, parameterised codebase driven by a publisher configuration layer. Swap the brand colours, logo, app name, App Store credentials, and backend API endpoints — and a fully branded standalone app was ready to publish. Each publisher's app appeared in the App Store under the publisher's own developer account and brand, with no visible connection to MagZine.

This architecture let TBox onboard new publishers rapidly, maintain a single codebase across all deployments, and ship feature updates to every publisher app simultaneously — while each publisher experienced their product as custom-built.

Technical depth

The PDF engine: open source, deeply extended

Magazine reading on mobile is a demanding problem. A single issue can be a 50MB+ PDF with high-resolution images, complex layouts, and embedded links. The commercially licensed PDF frameworks used in the main MagZine app added cost at every deployment — a problem at white-label scale where each publisher app needed the same capability.

For AppVilla, TBox chose an open-source PDF library as the foundation — then invested significant engineering time building it into a production-grade reading engine. The result was a lighter, more cost-effective foundation with custom capabilities that matched and in some areas exceeded the commercial alternatives.

Product 03 — Subscription service

MagZio: The Spotify for Dutch Magazines

In 2015, Spotify had proven that streaming subscription models could transform media consumption. Wob ten Hoeve applied the same logic to magazines: a flat monthly fee for unlimited reading across a curated catalogue. TBox built MagZio into the MagZine app as a subscription layer — €9.95 per month, 75+ titles, unlimited swapping.

The model was technically complex: a reading-time tracking engine that monitored minutes spent per publication and used that data to proportionally distribute subscription revenue to publishers — using cover price as a reference weighting. This publisher revenue engine was entirely custom, built by TBox.

What this partnership represents

At the time MagZine.nu was being built, iPad apps with this level of polish were genuinely rare in Europe. The digital magazine market was contested by global platforms — Zinio, Newsstand, Blendle — and most publishers outsourced their digital products to whoever was cheapest. MagZine.nu chose depth over convenience. TBox was there for every version of that choice, for ten years, across three products, on four platforms.
